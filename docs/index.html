18 October 2026 · Research direction and passive native contact audit

A visual goal still needs physical feedback

A goal image specifies a destination; physical feedback verifies whether a robot gets there. We describe a bounded decision role for TypeSafe Jev, distinguish it from action-conditioned world-model planning, and propose an independently scored reaching experiment. A separate passive audit reproduces fourteen previously inspected rigid-box arm episodes exactly and records 249,502 native ticks. Millisecond contact gaps hidden between stored observations become visible, while task outcomes remain unchanged. Complete inputs, traces, source snapshots and a verified downloader preserve every case. This is measurement work, not a new policy benchmark, PiPER shoe result or hardware qualification.

14/14 exact passive replays · 249,502 native ticks · visual models unbenchmarked

14 October 2026 · Frozen shape, load and attached-tool diagnostic

Give the gripper a fair test

A frozen 24-attempt PiPER diagnostic separates three rigid shapes, 30 g and 300 g loads, and mounted versus absent rake conditions. All attempts are valid; eight of twelve lightweight controls retain a direct lift while every 300 g attempt fails, including the small block. Removing the rake gains one narrow-shoe result and loses one block result, with no net completion gain. A verified settled reference, dense contact traces, unchanged drive fingerprint and independent scoring expose limits in the modeled grasp and force response. Recorded replays and runnable CPU code retain every failure. No autonomous tool removal, retrieval, learned skill or physical hardware qualification is claimed.

24/24 valid · 30 g: 8/12 lifts · 300 g: 0/12 · no net gain from removing rake

13 October 2026 · Live browser-native simulation with authored control and separate benchmark evidence

This authored rigid-shoe environment establishes no learned skill, calibrated material model or physical robot qualification.
